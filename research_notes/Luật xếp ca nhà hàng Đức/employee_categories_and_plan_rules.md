# Category-specific scheduling constraints and Dienstplan / shift-swap rules for restaurants in Germany (status: 7 Nov 2026)

Scope note: These are the rules that go on top of the general Arbeitszeitgesetz (ArbZG), which is covered elsewhere. "Statutory" means federal law; "CBA" means collective agreement (Tarifvertrag), which binds only members or businesses covered by a declaration of general applicability (Allgemeinverbindlicherklärung, AVE); "practice" means a guide or recommendation with no legal force. Fetched law texts are from gesetze-im-internet.de: the JArbSchG was last amended 23.10.2024 and the MuSchG on 22.12.2025.

---

## 1. Minors (Jugendarbeitsschutzgesetz, JArbSchG)

### Takeaway
Minors (15–17) are capped at 8 h/day, 40 h/week and 5 days/week. Their breaks are stricter, they need 12 h of rest between shifts, and they may only work 06:00–20:00. Restaurants get four sector exceptions: work until 22:00 for over-16s, an 11 h Schichtzeit cap instead of 10 h, and permission for Saturday, Sunday and most holiday work, subject to minimum free Saturdays/Sundays, a 5-day week, and absolute bans on 25 Dec, 1 Jan, Easter Sunday and 1 May. Days before and during Berufsschule (vocational school) add further limits.

### Cited Findings
- **Definitions (§2):** under 15 = "Kind" (child); 15 to under 18 = "Jugendlicher" (minor). Minors who still have full-time compulsory schooling (vollzeitschulpflichtig) are treated under the rules for children. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **Holiday jobs (§5 Abs. 4):** minors still under full-time compulsory schooling may work at most 4 weeks per calendar year during school holidays, and §§8–31 then apply. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§8 working time:** max 8 h/day and 40 h/week. Special rules exist for weeks with public holidays and for over-16s in agriculture at harvest time. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§9 Berufsschule:** the employer must release the minor for vocational school. No work may be scheduled before classes that start before 09:00. Further limits apply to days with more than 5 lessons and to block-teaching weeks. School attendance may not reduce pay. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§11 breaks (Ruhepausen):** breaks must be fixed in advance. Minimum break is 30 min for more than 4.5 up to 6 h of work, and 60 min for more than 6 h. A break only counts if it lasts at least 15 min. No more than 4.5 h of work in a row without a break. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§12 Schichtzeit (working time plus breaks):** max 10 h. **In the Gaststättengewerbe (hospitality) the cap is 11 h**; the same 11 h applies to agriculture, animal husbandry and construction sites, while underground mining is capped at 8 h. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§13 daily free time:** at least 12 consecutive hours off after the end of daily work. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§14 night rest:** work only between 06:00 and 20:00 (Abs. 1). **Over-16s in the Gaststätten- und Schaustellergewerbe may work until 22:00** (Abs. 2 Nr. 1); multi-shift operations allow until 23:00; bakeries may start at 05:00, or 04:00 for over-17s. **Abs. 4:** on the day before a Berufsschule day, the extensions in Abs. 2 Nr. 1–3 do not apply after 20:00 if school starts before 09:00. The exact wording is "nicht nach 20 Uhr beschäftigt werden, wenn der Berufsschulunterricht am Berufsschultag vor 9 Uhr beginnt". Abs. 7: performances are allowed until 23:00, followed by 14 h of free time. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§15 five-day week:** minors may work only 5 days per week, and the two rest days should be consecutive where possible. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§16 Saturdays:** Saturday work is banned except in listed sectors, which include hospitality. At least 2 Saturdays per month *should* stay free (a "soll" duty). A minor who works on a Saturday must get another free day in the same week. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§17 Sundays:** Sunday work is banned except in the sectors listed in Abs. 2; **Nr. 8 is "Gaststättengewerbe"**. "Jeder zweite Sonntag soll, mindestens zwei Sonntage im Monat müssen beschäftigungsfrei bleiben": every second Sunday should be free and at least 2 Sundays per month must be free. Abs. 3: the 5-day week must be kept by giving a free day on another workday of the same week without Berufsschule. If the business has a weekly closing day (Ruhetag), the free day may be that day. — [§17 JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/__17.html)
- **§18 public holidays:** no work on 24 and 31 Dec after 14:00 or on public holidays. The sector exceptions of §17 Abs. 2 (so hospitality) apply, **but never on 25 Dec, 1 Jan, Easter Sunday ("erster Osterfeiertag") or 1 May**. Work on a public holiday that falls on a weekday must be offset by a free day in the same or the following week. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§19 annual leave:** at least 30 working days if under 16 at the start of the calendar year, 27 if under 17, and 25 if under 18. Leave should fall in school holidays. Each Berufsschule day that falls during leave earns one extra leave day. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **§21a deviations by CBA** (or by a works agreement based on one): up to 9 h/day, 44 h/week and 5.5 days/week if the average over 2 months is 40 h; breaks reduced to 15 min; Schichtzeit extended by up to 1 h; work on 26 Saturdays a year or every Saturday with a free day in the same week; **Sunday work on up to 3 Sundays per month in hospitality and agriculture during the season**. Employers without a CBA may adopt these rules through a works agreement or, if there is no works council, a written agreement with the minor. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html)
- **Minimum wage:** minors under 18 (as defined in JArbSchG §2) without completed vocational training are not "employees" under the MiLoG, and the pay of Auszubildende (apprentices) is not set by the MiLoG. — [§22 MiLoG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/milog/__22.html)

### Inferences
- Suggested rule profile `MINOR_15_17`: maxDaily=8h; maxWeekly=40h; maxDaysPerWeek=5; maxShiftSpan (Schichtzeit)=11h for restaurant branches; minRest=12h; allowedWindow=06:00–20:00, extended to 22:00 if age ≥16 on the shift date (hospitality exception); breakRule={>4.5h→30min, >6h→60min, minBreakBlock=15min, maxContinuousWork=4.5h}; minFreeSundaysPerMonth=2 (hard), plus a soft warning if two Sundays in a row are worked; minFreeSaturdaysPerMonth=2 (soft "soll" warning); hard block on 25.12, 01.01, Easter Sunday, 01.05 and on 24.12/31.12 after 14:00; other public holidays are allowed but need a compensatory free day in the same or next week; weekend work requires a compensatory weekday off in the same week (the 5-day check covers this).
- Age must be computed **per shift date** from the date of birth: the 22:00 extension switches on at the 16th birthday, the whole profile switches off at the 18th, and leave entitlement is fixed by age on 1 January.
- Berufsschule calendar input per apprentice: (a) block any shift ending after 20:00 on the day before a school day that starts before 09:00; (b) block shifts before school when school starts before 09:00. The detailed §9 rules on crediting time also need modelling: a Berufsschule day with more than 5 lessons of 45 min counts as 8 h and no work is allowed that day (once per week); a block week of at least 25 h on at least 5 days counts as 40 h. These are well known but **were only partly confirmed by the fetched summary**, so verify against the full §9 text.
- §8 Abs. 2a (8.5 h on some days if other days of the same week are shorter than 8 h) and §11 Abs. 2 (break no earlier than 1 h after start and no later than 1 h before end) are commonly cited but were **not confirmed in the fetched summary**. Treat them as "verify before implementing".
- If the employer applies a hospitality CBA with §21a clauses, the profile values above should be configurable per branch or contract (e.g. 9 h/44 h, up to 3 Sundays per month in season).
- Children under 15, and minors still under full-time compulsory schooling outside the 4-week holiday allowance, should probably be blocked from scheduling entirely in a restaurant system.

### Gaps
- The Kinderarbeitsschutzverordnung (KindArbSchV, light work for 13–14-year-olds) was not researched. Whether any restaurant work qualifies is unclear, so a hard block is the safe design.
- I did not find whether any 2025/2026 reform of the JArbSchG is pending. The last amendment shown is 23.10.2024.

## 2. Pregnant and breastfeeding employees (Mutterschutzgesetz, MuSchG)

### Takeaway
Pregnant or breastfeeding employees aged 18+ are capped at 8.5 h/day and 90 h per double week (under-18s: 8 h and 80 h). They need 11 h of rest. Work between 20:00 and 06:00 is banned; 20:00–22:00 is possible only through the authority procedure in §28. Sunday and holiday work requires her express, revocable consent plus a substitute rest day. The protection periods (6 weeks before birth, 8–12 weeks after, and graded periods after a miscarriage from the 13th week) block scheduling entirely. The system should hold restriction flags with validity dates, not medical details.

### Cited Findings
- **§3 protection periods (Schutzfristen):** no employment in the last 6 weeks before the expected delivery date unless the woman expressly declares she wants to work; she can withdraw that at any time. No employment until 8 weeks after delivery; this becomes 12 weeks for premature or multiple births, or if a disability of the child is medically confirmed within 8 weeks. If the birth is early, the post-birth period is extended by the days lost before the birth. — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)
- **Miscarriage (current §3 text):** protection applies to miscarriages from the 13th week of pregnancy. The no-work period is 2 weeks from the 13th week, 6 weeks from the 17th week and 8 weeks from the 20th week. She may agree to work earlier and can revoke that at any time. — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)
- **§4 Mehrarbeit (overtime):** aged 18+: max 8.5 h/day and 90 h per double week, with Sundays counted. Under 18: 8 h/day and 80 h per double week. The monthly average may not exceed the contractually agreed weekly working time, and hours with multiple employers are added together. **§4 Abs. 2: at least 11 h of uninterrupted rest** after each working day. — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)
- **§5 night work:** "Der Arbeitgeber darf eine schwangere oder stillende Frau nicht zwischen 20 Uhr und 6 Uhr beschäftigen." Work until 22:00 is allowed only if the conditions of §28 are met. — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)
- **§28 authority approval for 20:00–22:00:** the employer applies to the supervisory authority. Conditions: (1) the woman expressly consents, (2) a medical certificate raises no objection, (3) there is no unacceptable risk from working alone (Alleinarbeit). The application must include the workplace risk assessment. She may be employed while the application is pending unless the authority rejects it or temporarily prohibits it. **If a complete application is not rejected within 6 weeks, it is deemed approved.** — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)
- **§6 Sundays and public holidays:** banned unless (1) she expressly consents, (2) an exception under the ArbZG applies, (3) she gets a substitute rest day each week following at least 11 h of uninterrupted night rest, and (4) Alleinarbeit risk is excluded. Her consent can be withdrawn at any time. — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)
- **§7 time off:** release for prenatal check-ups. Breastfeeding women, on request, get nursing time during the first 12 months after delivery: at least 2 × 30 min or 1 × 60 min per day, and more for shifts over 8 h. — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)
- **§27 employer duties:** notify the supervisory authority without delay once a pregnancy or breastfeeding is reported. Also notify if she is to work until 22:00, on Sundays or holidays, or in clocked (paced) work. Keep relevant records for at least 2 years, and do not pass the information on without authorisation. — [MuSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/muschg_2018/BJNR122810017.html)

### Inferences
- Suggested rule profile `MUSCHG_ACTIVE` (aged 18+): maxDaily=8.5h; maxPerDoubleWeek=90h (rolling 14-day window including Sundays); monthlyAvg ≤ contract weekly hours (important for part-timers and mini-jobbers); minRest=11h; hard window 06:00–20:00; window extended to 22:00 only if `ms_28_approval_status ∈ {approved, deemed_approved, pending_not_rejected}` and `ms_night_consent=true`; Sunday/holiday shifts only if `ms_sunday_consent=true` (revocable, date-stamped) plus an automatic substitute rest day that week; flag "no lone work" on shifts, i.e. at least one other employee must be on duty for the whole shift. For under-18s, combine with the minor profile (8 h, 80 h per double week).
- `MUSCHG_PROTECTION_PERIOD` (start and end dates): a hard block on all shifts unless the "pre-birth voluntary work" flag is set; that flag is only valid before the birth. Post-birth periods (8/12 weeks) are absolute, apart from the stillbirth exception.
- `BREASTFEEDING_UNTIL` (date ≤ birth + 12 months): reserve nursing breaks (2×30 or 1×60 min) as unproductive time within the shift.
- **Data minimisation (inference, GDPR principles, not specifically researched here):** pregnancy and breastfeeding status is health data (a special category under Art. 9 GDPR). The scheduler should store only the profile flag, validity dates, consent flags (date given/revoked), the §28 approval status and date, and the "no lone work" constraint. It should not store due-date reasoning, medical certificates or diagnoses. The medical certificate and authority correspondence belong in a separate, access-restricted HR file (§27 requires 2-year retention). Shift managers should see only "restricted availability profile M", not the reason. The same pattern should apply to the severe-disability flag (section 3). Role-based access and audit logs are advisable.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact in-force date of the miscarriage protection periods (I believe 1 June 2025, but that is unconfirmed) or what the 22.12.2025 amendment (Art. 13) changed.
- The detailed §11 bans on unsuitable work (e.g. heavy lifting, prolonged standing after the 5th month) were not researched; they matter for kitchen-station assignment.
- The GDPR / §26 BDSG legal bases were not researched in this pass; see the inference above.

## 3. Severely disabled employees (SGB IX)

### Takeaway
§207 SGB IX gives severely disabled employees a right, on request, to be released from Mehrarbeit (overtime). The scheduler needs an opt-in flag that hard-caps the daily length of shifts.

### Cited Findings
- §207 SGB IX, verbatim: "Schwerbehinderte Menschen werden auf ihr Verlangen von Mehrarbeit freigestellt." — [§207 SGB IX](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_9_2018/__207.html)

### Inferences
- Profile `SGB9_207_EXEMPT` (active only once the employee has requested it): a hard cap on daily working time. In the case law, "Mehrarbeit" in §207 is generally read as work beyond the 8 h workday under the ArbZG, not merely beyond the contractual hours (I recall BAG, 03.12.2002, 9 AZR 462/01; **not verified in this session**). So the default would be maxDaily=8h; a stricter contract-based cap could be offered as an option.
- Store only "§207 exemption requested: yes/no plus date". The disability itself (Grad der Behinderung, GdB) is health data and should be minimised in the same way as the MuSchG flag.
- Not a scheduling rule but related: severely disabled employees get extra leave (§208 SGB IX, 5 days for a 5-day week). This is well known but not verified here.

### Gaps
- The exact BAG definition of "Mehrarbeit" and whether night-work exemptions exist were not confirmed.

## 4. Minijob, minimum wage and short-term employment

### Takeaway
2026: minimum wage €13.90/h, Minijob limit €603/month (€7,236/year), which allows about 43 h/month. 2027: €14.60/h and €633/month (€7,596/year), still about 43 h/month. Occasional, unforeseeable overruns are allowed in up to 2 calendar months within 12 months, up to double the limit. Short-term employment (kurzfristige Beschäftigung) is capped at 3 months or 70 working days per calendar year. In hospitality, working time must be recorded within 7 days and kept for 2 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Minimum wage:** €13.90 gross/h from 1 Jan 2026 and €14.60 from 1 Jan 2027, set by the Fünfte Mindestlohnanpassungsverordnung (MiLoV5) of 5 Nov 2025, BGBl. 2025 I Nr. 268. — [MiLoV5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/milov5/BJNR10C0A0025.html)
- **Minijob limit 2026:** €603/month and €7,236/year, from 1 Jan 2026. The Übergangsbereich (Midijob range) runs from €603.01 to €2,000/month. — [IHK Schwerin](https://www.ihk.de/schwerin/recht/wirtschaftsrecht/arbeitsrecht/minijobs-3031906)
- **Minijob limit 2027:** €633/month and €7,596/year. Formula: minimum wage × 130 ÷ 3, rounded (based on 10 h/week at minimum wage); 14.60 × 130 / 3 = 632.67 → €633. This allows about 43 h/month. (Page published 7 Oct 2026.) — [Minijob-Zentrale Magazin](https://magazin.minijob-zentrale.de/minijob-grenze-2027)
- **Occasional unforeseeable overrun:** allowed in up to **2 calendar months within 12 months** (e.g. covering for a sick colleague), with earnings in those months up to **double the limit**: €1,206 in 2026 (allowing an annual maximum of €8,442) and €1,266 in 2027. — [IHK Schwerin](https://www.ihk.de/schwerin/recht/wirtschaftsrecht/arbeitsrecht/minijobs-3031906); [Minijob-Zentrale Magazin](https://magazin.minijob-zentrale.de/minijob-grenze-2027)
- **Kurzfristige Beschäftigung (short-term employment):** max "drei Monate oder 70 Arbeitstage" per calendar year, with all short-term jobs in the year added together. Above €603/month (2026) the employer must check whether the job is berufsmäßig (a main source of livelihood); if it is, it is subject to social insurance. Registered job-seekers cannot earn more than €603 in a short-term job. (Agriculture: 15 weeks or 90 days since 1 Jan 2026.) — [Minijob-Zentrale](https://www.minijob-zentrale.de/DE/01_minijobs/02_gewerblich/01_grundlagen/02_kurzfristige_gewerbliche_minijobs/01_zeitgrenzen/node.html); [IHK Schwerin](https://www.ihk.de/schwerin/recht/wirtschaftsrecht/arbeitsrecht/minijobs-3031906)
- **Recording duty (§17 MiLoG):** employers of mini-jobbers (§8 Abs. 1 SGB IV) **and** employers in the sectors listed in §2a SchwarzArbG must record the start, end and length of daily working time by the **7th calendar day** after the work, and keep the record for **at least 2 years**. — [§17 MiLoG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/milog/__17.html)
- **§2a SchwarzArbG Nr. 2 = "Gaststätten- und Beherbergungsgewerbe"** (restaurants and hotels): staff must carry ID and show it to customs inspectors, so restaurants are a listed sector. — [§2a SchwarzArbG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/schwarzarbg_2004/__2a.html)

### Inferences
- Max hours per month at minimum wage: 2026: 603 / 13.90 = 43.38 → 43 h (43 × 13.90 = €597.70; 43.5 h would be €604.65 and too much). 2027: 633 / 14.60 = 43.36 → 43 h. If a mini-jobber earns above minimum wage, cap = 603 / hourly rate. Taxable surcharges and one-off payments may also count towards the limit; the treatment of tax-free §3b surcharges should be checked with payroll.
- Profile `MINIJOB`: a soft monthly-hours cap computed from the hourly rate and the year's limit (config table: year → minimum wage → limit); a warning when planned plus actual earnings approach the limit; an "unforeseeable overrun" counter (max 2 months per rolling 12 months, max 2× the limit). Because restaurants are a §2a sector, time recording within 7 days applies to **all** staff, not only mini-jobbers. All ArbZG rules apply in full to mini-jobbers.
- Profile `KURZFRISTIG`: a counter of working days and calendar duration per calendar year, including prior short-term jobs with other employers (reported by the employee); hard stop at 70 days or 3 months.
- Minimum-wage validation: the hourly rate must be ≥ the statutory minimum for that year, except for minors without completed vocational training, apprentices, certain interns and long-term unemployed people in their first 6 months (§22 MiLoG). Hospitality CBA wages may be higher.

### Gaps
- The exemption thresholds in the MiLoDokV (recording not required above certain monthly salaries) were not verified.
- Whether the 3-month or the 70-day limit applies to a 5-day week was not answered on the Minijob-Zentrale page. The common reading is 3 months for ≥5 days/week and 70 days for fewer, but I did not verify that here.

## 5. Working students (Werkstudenten) and international students

### Takeaway
To keep Werkstudent social-insurance status, the student should work at most 20 h/week during the lecture period. More is allowed in semester breaks, and in term time if the work is mainly in evenings, nights or weekends, for no more than 26 weeks (182 days) in any 12 months. Non-EU students on a §16b permit have a 140-working-day account per year: a day of up to 4 h counts as half a day (so 280 half days), and alternatively a week counts as 2.5 days if it is at most 20 h in the lecture period or any week outside it.

### Cited Findings
- **20-hour rule:** the Werkstudent privilege applies only if the student works no more than 20 h/week during the lecture period (based on BSG, 11.11.2003, B 12 KR 24/03 R). Parallel jobs (Minijobs, short-term jobs, self-employment) are added together. **Exceptions:** semester breaks, or mainly evening/night/weekend work for no more than **26 weeks or 182 calendar days within any 12 months**. Only the health insurer decides whether the exception applies. Werkstudenten pay pension-insurance contributions and are exempt from unemployment-insurance contributions. — [Deutsches Studierendenwerk](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/themen/finanzierungsmoeglichkeiten/uebersicht-jobben/werkstudenten)
- **Non-EU students, §16b Abs. 3 AufenthG (current text):** the permit allows work of "bis zu 140 Arbeitstage im Jahr" (Arbeitstagekonto). Studentische Nebentätigkeiten (jobs at universities or research institutions) are not counted. Part-time work is counted in whichever way is more favourable to the student, decided per calendar week: Nr. 1, a day of up to 4 h counts as a half working day; Nr. 2, a whole calendar week counts as **2.5 working days**, regardless of how the hours are spread, (a) during the lecture period if at most 20 h/week, and (b) outside the lecture period. — [§16b AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__16b.html); [buzer.de §16b](https://www.buzer.de/16b_AufenthG.htm)
- In force since 1 March 2024. The limit runs per calendar year. Outside the lecture period the hours are unrestricted and each week counts as 2.5 days. Only days actually worked count; leave and sick days do not. — [Ausländerbehörde Hannover](https://www.hannover.de/content/download/971283/file/Info%20-%20Arbeitserlaubnis%20%C2%A716b.pdf)
- Make it in Germany (federal portal) describes this as "140 volle Tage oder 280 halbe Tage" (half day = up to 4 h), or alternatively up to 20 h/week in lecture periods. EU/EEA/Swiss students follow the same 20 h rule as German students. — [Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/de/studium-ausbildung/studieren-in-deutschland/arbeit)

### Inferences
- Profile `WERKSTUDENT`: needs a semester calendar per student (lecture periods and breaks). In lecture weeks: soft cap of 20 h/week. Count weeks above 20 h in term time and warn as the total approaches 26 weeks (182 days) in a rolling 12 months; the exception needs mainly evening/night/weekend hours, so the system could check the share of hours after about 18:00 or on Sat/Sun. In break weeks: only ArbZG limits.
- Profile `INTL_STUDENT_16B`: an "Arbeitstagekonto" ledger per calendar year, max 140.0 days. For each week, compute (a) the sum of daily units (≤4 h = 0.5, >4 h = 1.0) and (b) 2.5 if the week is in the lecture period with at most 20 h, or any week outside it; book min(a, b). Hard block once a shift would push the balance above 140. Jobs from other employers must be entered manually, since the limit is per person, not per employer. 52 weeks × 2.5 = 130 days, so a steady ≤20 h/week pattern always fits.
- Both profiles stack with the Minijob or Midijob profile if earnings are low.

### Gaps
- Whether the 26-week exception requires the hours themselves to fall in evenings and weekends, or just most of them, is decided by the health insurer case by case. No numeric "share" threshold was found.

## 6. Part-time and on-call work (TzBfG §12, Arbeit auf Abruf)

### Takeaway
For on-call contracts, shifts must be announced at least 4 days in advance or the employee need not work. If no weekly hours are agreed, 20 h/week applies; if no daily length is agreed, each call must be at least 3 consecutive hours. Extra hours above an agreed minimum are capped at +25%, and cuts below an agreed maximum at −20%. A CBA can deviate.

### Cited Findings
- §12 Abs. 1: the agreement must fix weekly and daily working time. If no weekly time is set, "gilt eine Arbeitszeit von 20 Stunden als vereinbart". If no daily time is set, the employer must use the employee for at least 3 consecutive hours each time. — [§12 TzBfG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tzbfg/__12.html)
- §12 Abs. 2: with an agreed minimum weekly time, the employer may call up to **25% more**; with an agreed maximum, it may call up to **20% less**. — [§12 TzBfG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tzbfg/__12.html)
- §12 Abs. 3: the employer must define reference hours and days within which work may be requested. The employee is only obliged to work if the shift is announced **at least 4 days in advance**. — [§12 TzBfG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tzbfg/__12.html)
- §12 Abs. 4–5: sick pay and holiday pay are based on the average working time of the last 3 months. — [§12 TzBfG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tzbfg/__12.html)
- §12 Abs. 6: a CBA may deviate from Abs. 1 and from the 4-day notice, even to the employee's disadvantage, if it itself sets daily/weekly working time and notice rules. Non-members within its scope may agree to apply it. — [§12 TzBfG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tzbfg/__12.html)

### Inferences
- Profile `ON_CALL`: fields contract_min_weekly_h / contract_max_weekly_h (default 20 h if both are empty), min_call_length=3h if no daily time is set, reference window (days and hours). Validation: (shift_start − published_at) ≥ 4 days. Otherwise mark the shift "not binding – employee acceptance required" and record acceptance. Weekly hours must fall within [min, min×1.25] or [max×0.8, max].
- "4 days" is commonly counted as 4 full calendar days between the notice day and the work day (e.g. notice on Monday for work on Saturday at the earliest). This counting method was **not verified**, so implement it conservatively.

### Gaps
- The exact way to count the 4 days (BGB §§187–193) was not confirmed in the sources fetched.

## 7. Publishing the shift plan (Dienstplan): notice, changes, works council

### Takeaway
There is no general statutory deadline for publishing a Dienstplan in Germany. The employer sets working time under its right to direct (§106 GewO) with reasonable discretion (billiges Ermessen). Courts commonly use **4 days** (borrowed from §12 TzBfG) as the minimum for announcing or changing shifts. CBAs and works agreements set the real deadlines: the Bavarian hospitality MTV (2006 text) requires posting by **Thursday for the following Mon–Sun week**. Where a works council exists, shift plans need its consent under §87(1) Nr. 2/3 BetrVG, and introducing the scheduling or time-tracking software itself is subject to co-determination under Nr. 6.

### Cited Findings
- "Für die Erstellung von Dienstplänen gibt es keine speziellen gesetzlichen Regelungen": the basis is §106 GewO with reasonable discretion. The BAG requires employers to consider protected family interests, which in practice means an early and reliable plan. Case law derives a 4-day minimum notice before a shift from §12 TzBfG, and changes also need 4 days except in urgent cases such as sudden sick calls (Tageskarte/dpa, 23 Oct 2023, quoting labour lawyer A. Bredereck). — [Tageskarte](https://www.tageskarte.io/zahlen/detail/bis-wann-muss-ein-dienstplan-erstellt-sein.html)
- ArbG Berlin, 05.10.2012, **28 Ca 10243/12**: minimum 4-day notice for changes to the Dienstplan. "Wenn der Plan steht, steht er": a published plan is binding, and changes need an unforeseeable event or an emergency. BAG, 13.06.2007, **5 AZR 849/06**: no right to a particular shift even after years of the same pattern. LAG Hessen, 13.01.2006, **3 Sa 2222/04**: refusing short-notice overtime does not justify dismissal without notice. (ERGO Rechtsportal, as of 01.01.2025.) — [ERGO Rechtsportal](https://www.ergo.de/de/rechtsportal/arbeitsrecht/arbeitszeit/dienstplan)
- "Es gibt keine bundeseinheitliche gesetzliche Frist" for the Dienstplan. The 4 days in §12 TzBfG are not automatically a minimum for every plan, but case law uses about 4 days as the lower limit of reasonableness. Changes after publication need consent or an urgent operational reason. Employees need not check the plan in their free time, and the employer must actively tell them about changes. **Practice guidelines (not law): about 2 weeks ahead in hospitality**, about 1 week under the TVöD, 2–4 weeks in care. Works agreements may set longer lead times and app-based notification. (Ordio, updated 21.10.2026; a software vendor, so secondary.) — [Ordio](https://www.ordio.com/insights/ratgeber/dienstplan-gesetz/)
- **§87 Abs. 1 BetrVG** (applies only where no statute or CBA already covers the matter): Nr. 2 "Beginn und Ende der täglichen Arbeitszeit einschließlich der Pausen" and its distribution over weekdays; Nr. 3 "vorübergehende Verkürzung oder Verlängerung der betriebsüblichen Arbeitszeit"; Nr. 5 leave principles and leave plan; **Nr. 6 "technischen Einrichtungen, die dazu bestimmt sind, das Verhalten oder die Leistung der Arbeitnehmer zu überwachen"**; Nr. 10 pay structure. If employer and works council disagree, the Einigungsstelle (conciliation board) decides (Abs. 2). — [§87 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html)
- LAG Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, **2 TaBVGa 5/15** (reported 7 Dec 2016): restaurants and hotels need works-council consent for Dienstpläne. Silence is not consent, the employer cannot set the council a deadline, and plans may not be made binding before consent. — [HOGAPAGE](https://www.hogapage.de/nachrichten/politik/recht/betriebsrat-muss-dienstplan-zustimmen/)
- **CBA example, MTV Gaststätten- und Beherbergungsgewerbe Bayern (26.05.2006, in force 01.01.2006), §7 X:** the schedule must be finalised and posted **by Thursday of the current week for the following Monday to Sunday**. Later changes for urgent operational reasons need agreement with the works council, or with the employee if there is none, and changes must be initialled by the employee. Protokollnotiz II: minimum contents include the working-time account balance and the employee's signature; no pencil entries. — [MTV Bayern 2006 (Böckler archive)](https://www.boeckler.de/pdf/ta_tv_Hotel_und_Gaststaettengewerbe_Bayern_MTV_2006.pdf)

### Inferences
- Plan-level settings per branch: `publish_lead_time` (default ≥ 4 days statutory floor; recommended ≥ 7–14 days; Bavarian CBA = "Thursday before the plan week"); `change_notice_min` = 4 days unless the change is flagged "urgent operational reason" (sick call) or "employee consent" with a timestamp; `requires_works_council_approval` (bool) → plan state machine: Draft → Submitted to Betriebsrat → Approved (or Einigungsstelle) → Published. Only after Published is the plan binding.
- Keep an immutable version history of published plans and changes (who, when, reason, consent/acknowledgement). This supports the 4-day evidence, the CBA initialling requirement, and §17 MiLoG / §16 ArbZG records.
- Notifications must be actively pushed (app, e-mail or SMS) and acknowledged. Do not rely on employees checking the plan.
- The software itself (time tracking, plan tracking, performance views) is a "technical device" under §87(1) Nr. 6 BetrVG, so a chain with works councils will need a works agreement before rollout. That is a requirement for the student team's project documentation.

### Gaps
- I did not find the current (post-2006) Bavarian MTV text or confirm whether §7 X is unchanged. MTV wording for other Länder (NRW, Hessen, Berlin, Hamburg, Baden-Württemberg, Niedersachsen) on Dienstplan lead times was not obtained; the summaries fetched for NRW and Sachsen did not cover working-time clauses.
- I found no BAG decision setting a general plan-publication deadline. The 4-day figure rests on lower-court decisions and analogy to §12 TzBfG.

## 8. Shift swaps (Schichttausch)

### Takeaway
No statute regulates shift swaps directly. A swap changes the Dienstplan, so it needs the employer's (supervisor's) approval. The employer stays responsible for compliance with the ArbZG, JArbSchG and MuSchG, which employees cannot waive even by consent. Best practice is a request → automated legal check for both employees → manager approval → re-publication workflow, with qualification matching and an audit trail. Where a works council exists, the general swap rules belong in a works agreement.

### Cited Findings
- Swaps between colleagues are common, but employees must coordinate with the supervisor, who must consent. For liability reasons, staff should not arrange swaps on their own (example from nursing, where the ward manager is responsible for the plan). Swapping staff must have the same qualification. — [ERGO Rechtsportal](https://www.ergo.de/de/rechtsportal/arbeitsrecht/arbeitszeit/dienstplan)
- The ArbZG is public law and is addressed to the **employer**, which is responsible for compliance. Exceeding maximum hours or shortening rest periods is generally not allowed **even with the employee's consent**. The 11 h rest under §5 ArbZG applies. Working-time changes are subject to §87 BetrVG and §315 BGB (reasonable discretion). (KomNet NRW, 09.01.2024, on working-time changes generally rather than swaps.) — [KomNet NRW](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/1349)
- Every planned shift must stay within the ArbZG limits, and deviations such as stepping in for someone (Einspringen) should be recorded in actual times. Works agreements may regulate substitution and stand-in rules. — [Ordio](https://www.ordio.com/insights/ratgeber/dienstplan-gesetz/)
- Bavarian hospitality MTV: changes to the plan for urgent operational reasons need agreement with the works council (or the employee) and the employee's initials. — [MTV Bayern 2006](https://www.boeckler.de/pdf/ta_tv_Hotel_und_Gaststaettengewerbe_Bayern_MTV_2006.pdf)

### Inferences
- Swap workflow: (1) employee A offers a shift or proposes a swap with B; (2) B accepts; (3) the system re-runs **all** rule profiles for both A and B over the affected period (daily and weekly max, 11 h or 12 h rest, minor or MuSchG windows, minor Sunday quota, §16b day account, Minijob earnings cap, Werkstudent 20 h, on-call +25%/−20%, CBA rest days, qualification/station); (4) manager approves, with a timestamp; (5) the plan is re-published and both are notified. A hard rule violation blocks approval; soft violations need a written justification.
- Accountability: approval by the employer's representative makes the swap an employer instruction, so the employer remains liable for ArbZG breaches. This is why the system should not allow "auto-approve" swaps that bypass checks. Work must be performed personally (general labour-law principle, §613 BGB; not verified in this session), which is another reason the employer must agree to a change of person.
- Pay effects: a swap can move hours into surcharge windows (night, Sunday, holiday) or across Minijob limits. The system should show the payroll impact before approval.

### Gaps
- I found no court decision or statute specifically on liability when employees swap shifts without approval. No primary source on DEHOGA guidance for swaps was found.

## 9. Public holidays by Bundesland and §3b EStG surcharges

### Takeaway
Germany has 9 nationwide public holidays; the rest vary by state, and some by municipality (Fronleichnam in parts of Sachsen and Thüringen, Mariä Himmelfahrt in about 1,700 Catholic Bavarian municipalities, the Augsburger Friedensfest in Augsburg city only). The place of work, not the place of residence, decides. Tax-free surcharges under §3b EStG: night 25% (40% for 00:00–04:00 if the shift starts before midnight), Sunday 50%, holidays and 31 Dec from 14:00 125%, 24 Dec from 14:00 / 25–26 Dec / 1 May 150%, on a base pay capped at €50/h.

### Cited Findings
- **Nationwide (2026 / 2027 dates):** Neujahr 01.01; Karfreitag 03.04.2026 / 26.03.2027; Ostermontag 06.04.2026 / 29.03.2027; 1. Mai; Christi Himmelfahrt 14.05.2026 / 06.05.2027; Pfingstmontag 25.05.2026 / 17.05.2027; Tag der Deutschen Einheit 03.10; 1. and 2. Weihnachtstag 25/26.12. — [DGB, as of 02.09.2026](https://www.dgb.de/service/ratgeber/feiertage/)
- **Regional:** Heilige Drei Könige 06.01 (BW, BY, ST); Internationaler Frauentag 08.03 (BE, MV); Ostersonntag and Pfingstsonntag (explicitly BB only); Fronleichnam 04.06.2026 / 27.05.2027 (BW, BY, HE, NW, RP, SL; in SN only certain Catholic communities in the Sorbian area of Landkreis Bautzen; in TH only Landkreis Eichsfeld and certain communities in the Unstrut-Hainich-Kreis and Wartburgkreis); Augsburger Friedensfest 08.08 (Augsburg city only); Mariä Himmelfahrt 15.08 (SL; in BY only about 1,700 mainly Catholic municipalities, not the other ~350); Weltkindertag 20.09 (TH); Reformationstag 31.10 (BB, HB, HH, MV, NI, SN, ST, SH, TH); Allerheiligen 01.11 (BW, BY, NW, RP, SL); Buß- und Bettag 18.11.2026 / 17.11.2027 (SN). The place of work counts, not residence. — [DGB](https://www.dgb.de/service/ratgeber/feiertage/)
- **§3b EStG tax-free surcharges** (percent of base pay, Grundlohn, for work actually performed): night work (20:00–06:00) 25%; Sunday 50%; 31 Dec from 14:00 and statutory holidays 125%; 24 Dec from 14:00, 25 and 26 Dec and 1 May 150%. If night work starts before midnight, 00:00–04:00 earns 40%, and work in that window on the day after a Sunday or holiday still counts as Sunday or holiday work. Sunday and holiday work means the full day 00:00–24:00. Base pay is capped at **€50/h**. Holidays are determined by the law at the **place of work** (Arbeitsstätte). — [§3b EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__3b.html)
- CBA surcharges are separate from these tax-free maxima. Examples: MTV Hessen (from 01.01.2024) Sunday surcharge 25% (previously 0%) and night 25% for 23:00–06:00 (previously 20% for 01:00–06:00). — [NGG flyer MTV Hessen 2024](https://suedwest.ngg.net/fileadmin/Landesbezirke/Suedwest/Dateien_und_PDFs/Branchen/2023/HoGa/HoGa_Flugblatt_-_MTV_2024.pub.pdf). MTV Bayern 2006: night 20% for 24:00–07:00 if at least 24 such days per year; no Sunday or holiday percentage, but a compensatory free day within 5 weeks for work on a weekday holiday (§9). — [MTV Bayern 2006](https://www.boeckler.de/pdf/ta_tv_Hotel_und_Gaststaettengewerbe_Bayern_MTV_2006.pdf)

### Inferences
- Data model: `HolidayCalendar(region_code)`, where the region code is the Bundesland plus an optional municipality or district (needed for Augsburg, Bavarian Mariä Himmelfahrt municipalities, and Sorbian Sachsen / Eichsfeld Thüringen). Each `Branch` links to one calendar. Movable feasts are computed from Easter. Calendars for 2026–2027 can be seeded from the DGB list.
- For a multi-state chain the same date can be a holiday in one branch and a normal day in another (e.g. 31.10 in Hamburg vs. NRW; 01.11 in NRW vs. Hamburg). An employee lent to another branch follows that branch's calendar (place of work) for holiday rules (JArbSchG §18, MuSchG §6) and for §3b surcharges.
- Surcharge engine: split each shift into segments (20–24, 00–04 if started before midnight, 04–06, Sunday, holiday, 24/31 Dec after 14:00) and apply max(CBA rate, contract rate). The §3b percentages are only the **tax-free ceilings**, not a legal obligation to pay surcharges; payment obligations come from the CBA or contract (and the ArbZG compensation duty for night work, covered elsewhere).
- Social-insurance exemption for these surcharges is limited to base pay of €25/h (SvEV), unlike the €50/h tax cap. This is well known but **not verified in this session**.

### Gaps
- Official Länder holiday laws were not individually checked; the DGB list notes it is without guarantee. One-off holidays (such as Berlin's 8 May 2025) did not appear for 2026/2027.

## 10. Hospitality collective agreements (DEHOGA / NGG): typical clauses and general applicability

### Takeaway
Hospitality CBAs are regional (per Land). Typical Manteltarifvertrag (MTV, framework agreement) clauses include a 5-day week with **2 consecutive days off (48 h)**, which may be split for operational reasons; a minimum number of free Sundays (Bayern: 12 per year); split shifts with a break preferably no longer than 3 h; rest reduced to 10 h; monthly hour caps (Bayern: 200 h); overtime surcharges (Bayern: 35%); and plan-posting rules. CBAs normally bind only DEHOGA members with NGG-member employees. General applicability (AVE) is the exception, but Schleswig-Holstein declared its hospitality wage agreement generally binding from 01.11.2024.

### Cited Findings
- **MTV Bayern (signed 26.05.2006, in force 01.01.2006):** regular working time 8 h/day, 39 h/week or 169 h/month, spread over 5 days; monthly cap 200 h; flexible model 30–48 h/week and 5–10 h/day over 12 months; **minimum rest 10 h** (§7 VII); **§10: 48 consecutive hours off** for 5-day-week employees, which may be split into 2 × 24 h for urgent operational reasons; on request, one free day should fall on a Sunday within 4 weeks; **at least 12 free Sundays per calendar year**; §7 VI split shift: the break "soll … zusammenhängend nicht mehr als 3 Stunden betragen" (should not exceed 3 h in one piece), and only breaks during which the employee may leave the premises count; overtime surcharge 35% (of 1/169 of monthly pay); night 20% for 24:00–07:00 (if at least 24 days per year); Dienstplan posted by Thursday for the next week. — [MTV Bayern 2006](https://www.boeckler.de/pdf/ta_tv_Hotel_und_Gaststaettengewerbe_Bayern_MTV_2006.pdf)
- **IHK fact sheet on CBA rules for hospitality apprentices (region not stated, "Stand: April 2026"):** says the MTV is allgemeinverbindlich; **two rest days per week**, of which one must be given and the second may be given as two half days or collected at the end of the season; an extra free day if an apprentice works on a weekday public holiday (Mon–Fri); training businesses must keep a Dienstplan. — [IHK fact sheet](https://www.ihk.de/blueprint/servlet/resource/blob/1676810/36c746af3fd277b8a6b35c33b0f5547a/merkblatt-tarifliche-regelungen-im-hotel-und-gaststaettengewerb-data.pdf)
- **Schleswig-Holstein:** the state government declared the hospitality Lohn- und Gehaltstarifvertrag (wage agreement) allgemeinverbindlich, retroactive to 1 Nov 2024; +€200 from 1 May 2025 (around €15/h); covers about 80,000 employees regardless of union membership; runs until at least end of June 2026 (article of 28 Mar 2025). — [Tageskarte](https://www.tageskarte.io/zahlen/detail/schleswig-holstein-15-euro-stundenlohn-ab-mai-im-gastgewerbe.html)
- **NRW:** MTV in force since 01.05.2016; regular working time 169 h/month; leave 28 days from age 19, 30 from age 36. — [Tarifregister NRW summary](https://www.tarifregister.nrw.de/material/gaststaetten2.pdf). **Sachsen:** MTV from 01.07.2005; 173.5 h/month regular working time; wage agreement 01.01.2024–31.12.2026. — [Sachsen Tarif summary](https://arbeit.sachsen.de/download/Hotel_und_Gaststaettengewerbe_0924.pdf). **Hessen:** new MTV from 01.01.2024 (Sunday surcharge 25%, night 25% for 23:00–06:00, leave 28–31 days). — [NGG flyer](https://suedwest.ngg.net/fileadmin/Landesbezirke/Suedwest/Dateien_und_PDFs/Branchen/2023/HoGa/HoGa_Flugblatt_-_MTV_2024.pub.pdf)
- §21a JArbSchG lets hospitality CBAs widen minor rules (e.g. up to 3 Sundays per month in season). §12 Abs. 6 TzBfG lets CBAs change the on-call 4-day notice. — [JArbSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/jarbschg/BJNR009650976.html); [§12 TzBfG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tzbfg/__12.html)

### Inferences
- Model CBAs as an optional, per-branch "Tarif profile" layered on top of statutory profiles: `weekly_rest_days=2 (consecutive, split allowed)`, `min_free_sundays_per_year=12`, `max_monthly_hours=200`, `min_rest_hours=10` (only valid if the CBA lawfully reduces the ArbZG 11 h), `split_shift_max_break=3h (soft)`, `overtime_surcharge=35%`, `night_surcharge=…`, `publish_deadline="Thursday 23:59 before plan week"`. A restaurant chain that is not a DEHOGA member and is not covered by an AVE is legally bound only by statute and contracts. Many chains still use the regional CBA by reference in contracts, so the profile should be switchable per branch.
- Wage-validation rule for SH branches (if the AVE is still in force): CBA wage table minimums apply to all employees.

The BMAS AVE register was not fetched; the SH declaration runs "at least until end of June 2026", and whether it was renewed or extended is unknown. The IHK fact sheet that says "MTV allgemeinverbindlich" does not name its region.
- The current Bavarian MTV (whether the 2006 text was replaced) and MTV texts for other Länder were not obtained. Split-shift limits ("max one split per day") and "2 days off" clauses outside Bayern are unverified.
- Not researched here (out of scope, but relevant): the planned ArbZG reform from daily to weekly maximum hours announced in the 2025 coalition agreement, and its status as of Nov 2026.
