# Shift-scheduling / workforce-management software for German restaurants, and algorithmic approaches to compliance-aware scheduling (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: research done 2026-11-07. Evidence labels used below:
- **[Official doc]**: vendor help center or developer documentation (strongest evidence of actual behaviour)
- **[Official marketing]**: vendor product, pricing or launch page (a claim, often vague)
- **[Third-party]**: directory, review or comparison site (weakest; may be vendor-supplied text)
- **[Not found]**: searched, but no statement on the official pages fetched. This means "not verified", not "feature absent".

Several help centers (Quinyx helpdocs, Timefold GitHub) could not be fully fetched: the article bodies did not render, or robots.txt blocked the fetch. Where only article *titles* were visible, this is stated explicitly.

## 1. Products: what the market does for compliance and eligibility (ArbZG checks, qualifications/certificates, employee app, swaps, approvals, availability, time tracking, pricing)

### Takeaway
Every relevant product shows employees their shifts in a mobile app and handles open shifts and availability. Most can be configured with working-time rules, but they usually **warn** rather than **block**. The documentation is explicit about this for Planday ("cannot block you"). Only Deputy states it can block a *swap* that would cause unwanted overtime or a compliance problem. **No product's official pages verified a check of certificate expiry, such as the §43 IfSG Belehrung, that prevents scheduling.** The closest documented mechanism is Timefold's time-limited skills (`validityDateTimeSpans`) combined with the hard constraint "Required skill missing". German gastronomy specialists (gastromatic, e2n, Papershift) market legal compliance only vaguely ("Arbeitsrecht wird automatisch mitgedacht"). The enterprise tools (Quinyx, Planday, Shiftbase) document configurable rule engines. A student system with a transparent, explainable ArbZG check plus a document-expiry eligibility check would therefore not just duplicate the SMB gastro tools.

**Planday** (official help center)
- Working-time rule structures: "Working hours: Shift duration; working hours in a period of time"; "Working days: working days in a calendar week; consecutive working days; working days in a period of time; repeating Shift type series in a period of time"; "Time off: group of days off in a period of time; hours off between shifts; continuous hours off in a period of time; time off after consecutive shifts". Breaks and minors are not mentioned. — [Planday: Working time rules](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/5046320-working-time-rules)
- "Rules are guidelines and only trigger warnings": a working time rule "cannot block you from creating or assigning a Shift." When a warning appears, the planner can ignore the rule, edit the shift to comply, or cancel the change. — [Planday: Working time rules](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/5046320-working-time-rules)
- Rules are scoped by employee group, employee type and shift type (all shift types by default; some can be excluded). Path: Settings > Schedule > Working time rules. If a needed rule structure is missing, the customer must contact support, so the set of rule types is fixed and parameterised. — [Planday: Working time rules](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/5046320-working-time-rules)
- Use-case examples: max shift duration of 11 h; max 6 working days per week; max 6 consecutive shifts; max 48 h/week for FOH staff; minimum 28 h/week for receptionists. **The "week" runs for 7 days from the shift's date, not Monday to Sunday** (a rolling window). — [Planday: Working time rules use cases](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/30472-working-time-rules-use-cases)
- Open shifts appear red until an admin or schedule manager approves an applicant. Only employees in the relevant employee group, or who meet the position's requirements, can request them. Eligible employees get push notifications when a shift matches their availability, has no conflicts and fits their group. — [Planday: Open shifts, shift requests and shift swaps](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/30399-open-shifts-shift-requests-and-shift-swaps)
- Swap (two employees exchange), hand over (transfer to a colleague), sell (offered as an open shift, shown yellow). Swaps are only allowed within the same employee group and department. The shift stays with the original employee until the colleague agrees and a manager approves. Approval is configured with an hours cutoff in Settings > Schedule > Swap and Availability. For example, 48 means only swaps within 2 days need approval, 0 means no approval, and a very large number means always approve. ### Inferences
- **Warn-by-default is the dominant market pattern.** Planday is explicitly warn-only, Shiftbase flags, and Quinyx warns and allows overrides. Hard blocking appears mainly for employee self-service actions (Deputy swaps) and possibly Quinyx's "Overtime limit can't be exceeded" (title only). A sensible design for the student project: **manager edits → warn (with override + reason); employee self-service (swap, claim open shift) → block if any hard rule fails.**
- **Certificate/training expiry gating appears to be a gap in the German gastro SMB tools.** gastromatic and e2n document personnel files and uploads but not expiry-driven eligibility. Modelling the §43 IfSG Belehrung and other mandatory trainings as **skills with validity windows** (the Timefold pattern) and treating "required, valid on the shift date" as a hard eligibility rule is a realistic differentiator for the student project.
- Planday's rolling 7-day "week" shows that rule definitions need an explicit **window semantics** parameter (calendar week vs rolling window vs 6-month average). This matters because ArbZG §3 uses an averaging period.
- Rule scoping (Planday: by employee group/type/shift type; Quinyx: by region/contract/agreement/age/role/skill; Shiftbase: ruleset per contract type) indicates the data model should attach **rule sets to contract types / employee categories** (e.g., minors under JArbSchG, Minijob, tarifgebunden vs not), not hard-code them globally.

### Gaps
- **When I Work** was not researched (time budget). It is US-centric and likely low-relevance for Germany.
- **German ArbZG-specific checks** (11 h rest, 6 h/9 h break rule, 10 h max/day, 15 free Sundays/year, JArbSchG) were **not verified on any vendor's official help pages**. Papershift, gastromatic, Factorial and Quinyx only make general marketing claims, and Shiftbase documents only the Dutch law. Most of these vendors are known to market "ArbZG-konform" planning, but no help-center article confirming exact rule coverage was retrieved.
- Quinyx help-center article bodies (Violations Panel, audit logs, mobile-action compliance, Minijobber limit) could not be fetched, so only titles are confirmed.
- Pricing for Planday, Quinyx, Shiftbase, Papershift, Deputy, Factorial and e2n was not verified. gastromatic's per-unit basis (per location vs per employee) is unclear.
- Personio: whether a native "Personio Shift Planning" module exists in 2026 (in addition to partner apps) was not verified.
- No product was found to document scheduling-time blocking based on an expired §43 IfSG certificate.
- German gastro-focused competitors seen in search results but not researched: Ordio, shyftplan, Planerio, clockin, Staffomatic.

## 2. UX patterns: how violations are explained, override with justification, audit log

### Takeaway
The documented UI patterns are: (a) an inline warning dialog at create/edit time with the options **ignore / fix / cancel** (Planday); (b) a **per-shift icon with hover explanation** plus a **global violations counter** (Shiftbase); (c) a **violations panel**, rule filters, and **audit logs for rules and rule sets** (Quinyx, titles and marketing only). **Requiring a written justification on override was not found documented** by any vendor. The student project can add it cheaply, and it is good practice for German labour-inspection audits.

### Cited Findings
- Planday: a warning appears when creating or modifying a shift that breaks a rule. The user can **ignore the rule, edit the shift to comply, or cancel**. Rules "cannot block". Each rule has a unique **name**, which is what the planner sees. — [Planday: Working time rules](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/5046320-working-time-rules)
- Shiftbase: "Violations" counter in the schedule status bar, an exclamation mark on the offending shift, and hover to see which conflict/violation it is. Checks run immediately on change. — [Shiftbase help: Dutch Working Hours Act](https://help.shiftbase.com/dutch-working-hours-act)
- Quinyx: "Real-time warnings and rule filters during scheduling"; managers get "clear warnings when actions may breach compliance"; managers can override; "Complete audit-ready scheduling history"; exportable compliance reports for Legal/HR. — [Quinyx: Compliance checker](https://www.quinyx.com/optimize-your-business/compliance-checker)
- Quinyx help center has separate articles titled "Violations Panel", "Audit logs - Rule" and "Audit logs - Rule set" (titles only), which implies changes to the *rule configuration itself* are audited. — [Quinyx help: Schedule compliance rules](https://quinyx.helpdocs.io/l/en/article/h01dkne4mj)
- Deputy: managers see a trade's cost or savings before approving. Non-compliant swaps can be blocked. — [Deputy: Shift swapping](https://www.deputy.com/features/shift-swapping)
- gastromatic: "Planungskonflikte direkt im Dienstplan sichtbar" (conflicts visible directly in the roster). — [gastromatic (DE homepage)](https://www.gastromatic.com/de/)

### Inferences
- Recommended violation object for the student system, combining the patterns above: `{ruleId, ruleName, legalBasis (e.g. "§5 Abs.1 ArbZG"), severity (HARD/SOFT/INFO), employeeId, affectedShiftIds, measured value vs limit (e.g. "rest 9h15m < 11h"), suggestedFix (e.g. "move start to 08:00 or assign X/Y who are eligible")}`. Show it as a per-shift badge, a hover tooltip, a side "violations panel" with filters, and a global counter.
- Override flow: allow managers (role-based) to publish a schedule with HARD warnings only after entering a **free-text reason**. Store `{who, when, ruleId, shiftId, reason}` in an append-only audit table. Also audit changes to rule parameters (Quinyx-style "Audit logs - Rule set"). This is cheap to build and a visible "beyond market" feature for a thesis.
- "Suggested fix" can be computed without an optimizer: e.g., list eligible employees for a shift (skill valid, available, no rest/max-hour violation if assigned). This is the same filter Planday uses to target open-shift notifications.

### Gaps
- No vendor documentation was found showing that violation messages cite the **legal paragraph** (e.g., §5 ArbZG) or propose an automatic fix. This may exist in some products but was not verified.
- No vendor documented **mandatory justification text** on override.

## 3. Algorithms: validating vs generating schedules; hard vs soft constraints; CP-SAT, Timefold, ILP, metaheuristics; fairness

### Takeaway
Treat the problem as two separate features over one shared rule model: **(a) validation** of a manually built roster, which is deterministic rule evaluation, simple and fully testable, and enough for an MVP; and **(b) auto-generation**, an instance of the Nurse Rostering / Personnel Scheduling Problem, NP-hard in general and studied for decades. For (b), off-the-shelf solvers make a student implementation feasible. **Google OR-Tools CP-SAT** has official employee-scheduling examples and a richer `shift_scheduling_sat.py` showing hard/soft sequence and sum constraints with penalties. **Timefold Solver** (the OptaPlanner successor) has an employee-scheduling quickstart and a commercial model with explicit hard/soft constraint semantics, including time-limited skills. Legal rules should be hard constraints; fairness and preferences soft constraints with weights.

### Cited Findings

**Literature**
- Van den Bergh, J., Beliën, J., De Bruecker, P., Demeulemeester, E., De Boeck, L. (2013), "Personnel scheduling: A literature review", *European Journal of Operational Research* 226(3): 367–385, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2012.11.029. It classifies the personnel scheduling literature by problem setting and technical features (tables of classifications) and identifies research trends. — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v226y2013i3p367-385.html); [KU Leuven Lirias](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/231816)

It reviews solution approaches "ranging from operations research to artificial intelligence" and argues good rosters balance workloads and respect staff preferences: "A high quality roster can lead to a more contented and thus more effective workforce." — [University of Stirling record](https://www.stir.ac.uk/research/hub/publication/693783)

**Google OR-Tools CP-SAT**
- Official employee-scheduling example: Boolean variable `shifts[(n, d, s)]` = nurse n works shift s on day d. Constraints: each shift each day is assigned to exactly one nurse; each nurse works at most one shift per day; shifts are spread as evenly as possible (with 9 shifts and 4 nurses, each works at least 2 and at most one extra). A second example (5 nurses, 7 days, 3 shifts) handles **shift requests via the objective** (maximise requests met; 13 of 20 met in the sample), not as hard constraints. Solver: CP-SAT. Languages: Python, C++, Java, C#. The page links to the more sophisticated `shift_scheduling_sat.py`. — [Google OR-Tools: Employee Scheduling](https://developers.google.com/optimization/scheduling/employee_scheduling)
- `shift_scheduling_sat.py` (8 employees, 3 weeks, shifts Off/Morning/Afternoon/Night; `work[e, s, d]` Booleans):
  - **Hard**: exactly one shift per day per employee (`add_exactly_one`); fixed assignments.
  - **Soft requests**: weighted Booleans; negative weight = reward, positive = penalty.
  - **`add_soft_sequence_constraint`**: bounds consecutive runs of a shift with `hard_min`, `soft_min`, `soft_max`, `hard_max`, `min_cost`, `max_cost`. Outside the hard bounds is forbidden; outside the soft bounds incurs a linear penalty proportional to the distance. Example: night shifts soft 2–3, hard 1–4.
  - **`add_soft_sum_constraint`**: bounds the weekly count of a shift with the same four bounds and costs. Example: rest days per week hard 1–3, soft target 2.
  - **Penalised transitions**: cost 0 means a forbidden transition (night→morning, which is exactly a rest-period rule); positive cost means a penalised transition (afternoon→night, cost 4).
  - **Cover constraints**: per day and shift, assigned staff ≥ demand; the excess is an integer variable penalised per shift type (weights 2, 2, 5).
  - **Objective**: minimise the weighted sum of penalties, request weights and excess.
  — [OR-Tools GitHub: shift_scheduling_sat.py](https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/stable/examples/python/shift_scheduling_sat.py)

### Inferences
- **Validation (a)** is a pure function: `validate(schedule, employees, rules, period) -> List<Violation>`. Each rule is a small evaluator over an employee's sorted shift timeline: rest between consecutive shifts, sum of hours in a window, break length vs shift length, count of Sundays worked in the year, minor-specific limits, and skill validity on the shift date. Complexity is linear in the number of shifts per employee. It is easy to unit-test, and it is all the MVP needs.
- **Generation (b)** should **reuse the same rule definitions** (as Quinyx does with auto-assign). In CP-SAT, each rule maps to a constraint pattern:
  - 11 h rest: a forbidden pairs/transition constraint, as with night→morning.
  - Max hours in a window: a linear sum ≤ limit.
  - Consecutive days: a sequence constraint.
  - Sundays: a count over Sunday variables.
  - Expired certificate: the variable is simply not created, or is fixed to 0, when the skill is invalid on that date. This is the cheapest way to enforce eligibility.
  - Coverage per branch/role/time slot: ≥ demand (hard) or a penalised shortfall (soft, which avoids infeasibility).
- **Hard vs soft split (recommended)**:
  - Hard: ArbZG/JArbSchG limits, skill/certificate validity, approved absences, no overlaps, max one shift per person per time slot.
  - Soft: preferences/Wunschfrei, fairness (balanced Sundays, weekends, late/night shifts, total hours vs contract), minimal overstaffing, continuity/same branch.
  - Coverage: usually soft with a high penalty, so the solver returns the "least bad" plan plus explicit understaffing, instead of "infeasible".
- **Fairness metrics** to implement as soft terms or report KPIs: per employee per period, the count of Sundays, weekend days, late/night shifts, public holidays, and |planned hours − contract hours|. Minimise the max−min spread or the sum of deviations from the mean. The OR-Tools tutorial's "spread evenly" and Timefold's "balancing time worked and shift counts" are concrete reference implementations.
- **ILP / MIP** formulations (same Boolean variables, linear constraints) are equivalent in modelling style for this size. CP-SAT is generally the pragmatic default because Google's own examples target it. **Metaheuristics** (local search, tabu, simulated annealing, used inside Timefold) scale better to large instances and support incremental "repair" after a change, which suits re-planning after a swap or sick call.
- **Problem size**: a restaurant chain branch with roughly 10–40 staff over a 1–4 week horizon with 2–4 shift types is small for CP-SAT. The OR-Tools example (8 employees × 21 days × 4 shift types) is in the same order of magnitude, so a student team can expect seconds-to-minutes solve times. This is an estimate, not benchmarked here.
- **Choice for a student team**: Java/Spring stack → Timefold (Java-native, constraint streams, built-in score explanations) or OR-Tools Java. Python/FastAPI stack → OR-Tools CP-SAT Python, the best-documented path.

## 4. Practical architecture notes: rules as data (ArbZG reform), Europe/Berlin time zone and DST, testing

### Takeaway
German working-time law is **actively changing in 2026**. A BMAS draft (June 2026) would let *collective agreements* replace the daily limit with a 48 h weekly maximum averaged over 12 months. It would allow rest periods in hospitality to be shortened by up to one hour, and make same-day electronic time recording mandatory. As of October 2026 it was still an internal draft disputed in the coalition, with a target start of 1 Jan 2027. The system must therefore model rules as **versioned, parameterised configuration per agreement/contract type with validity dates**, not hard-coded constants. Times must be stored as instants with Europe/Berlin zone rules, so that shifts crossing midnight and DST changes compute correct durations and rest periods.

### Cited Findings

**ArbZG reform status and content**
- The Federal Cabinet approved a plan (as part of its tourism strategy) to replace the daily 8 h maximum with a weekly maximum "im Einklang mit der europäischen Arbeitszeitrichtlinie". BMAS was to draft a bill with the social partners, and the government intended to pass it in 2026. DGB announced resistance. The article is dated 30 Jan 2026. — [PTA in Love (30 Jan 2026)](https://www.pta-in-love.de/?p=135256)
- Leaked BMAS draft (reported 18 June 2026): the 8-hour day generally stays, with broader exceptions. Collective/works agreements could replace the daily limit with a weekly limit of **max 48 h on an annual average**, with health-protection rules. **Start, end and duration of working time would be recorded electronically each day.** Dehoga supports this; unions oppose it. The draft was at an early stage and still in the ministry. — [HOGAPAGE (18 Jun 2026)](https://www.hogapage.de/nachrichten/politik/branchenpolitik/entwurf-zur-arbeitszeit-reform-mehr-flexibilit%C3%A4t-f%C3%BCr-das-gastgewerbe/)
- The weekly maximum would apply only to tariff-bound firms. Firms without a collective agreement keep the 8 h day (up to 10 h with compensation). The **compensation period would drop from 6 to 4 months**. Electronic same-day recording is required, with transition periods of 2 years (<250 employees) and 5 years (<50 employees). Firms with ≤10 employees must record, but not electronically. The planned start is **1 January 2027**, and many changes are expected. — [Handwerksblatt (1 Jul 2026)](https://www.handwerksblatt.de/themen-specials/arbeitszeit-erfassen-aber-wie/neues-arbeitszeitgesetz-das-steht-drin)
- As of October 2026: still a BMAS Referentenentwurf ("interne Arbeitsfassung", "Hausabstimmung"), disputed in the coalition. 48 h/week averaged over 12 months only via collective agreement plus special health protection. **Rest periods could be shortened by up to one hour in health/care, hospitality/accommodation ("Gastgewerbe"), transport, broadcasting, agriculture.** Electronic same-day recording, with exceptions via collective agreement or for very small businesses. — [CMS Hasche Sigle: Update Arbeitsrecht Oktober 2026](https://cms.law/de/deu/publication/update-arbeitsrecht-oktober-2026/arbeitsrechtliche-reformen-im-fokus-was-arbeitgeber-jetzt-wissen-muessen)
- Source conflict: headlines such as "Kabinett winkt wöchentliche Höchstarbeitszeit durch" (Jan 2026) can be misread as the law being passed. The later sources (Jun–Oct 2026) show only a plan approval plus an internal ministry draft, with no bill passed by the Bundestag. — [PTA in Love](https://www.pta-in-love.de/?p=135256) vs [CMS (Oct 2026)](https://cms.law/de/deu/publication/update-arbeitsrecht-oktober-2026/arbeitsrechtliche-reformen-im-fokus-was-arbeitgeber-jetzt-wissen-muessen)

**Market evidence for "rules as data"**
- Planday: rules are instances of fixed parameterised structures, scoped by employee group/type/shift type. — [Planday: Working time rules](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/5046320-working-time-rules)
- Shiftbase: a ruleset is assigned per contract type. — [Shiftbase help](https://help.shiftbase.com/dutch-working-hours-act)
- Quinyx: rules are configured by region/contract/agreement/age/role/skill, with "agreement-based validation" and "region-specific compliance templates"; rule and rule-set changes are audit-logged (titles). — [Quinyx: Compliance checker](https://www.quinyx.com/optimize-your-business/compliance-checker); [Quinyx help titles](https://quinyx.helpdocs.io/l/en/article/h01dkne4mj)

**Time zone / DST (Java `java.time` reference behaviour)**
- `ZonedDateTime.of(LocalDateTime, ZoneId)`: "In the case of an overlap, when clocks are set back, there are two valid offsets. This method uses the earlier offset typically corresponding to 'summer'." "In the case of a gap, when clocks jump forward, there is no valid offset. Instead, the local date-time is adjusted to be later by the length of the gap." `withEarlierOffsetAtOverlap()` / `withLaterOffsetAtOverlap()` select the offset explicitly. `ofStrict(...)` throws `DateTimeException` if the offset is invalid for the zone. — [Oracle Java 21 API: ZonedDateTime](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/time/ZonedDateTime.html)

### Inferences
- **Rule model (data, not code)**: `RuleDefinition {id, type (MAX_HOURS_WINDOW | MIN_REST_BETWEEN | BREAK_BY_DURATION | MAX_CONSECUTIVE_DAYS | MIN_FREE_SUNDAYS_YEAR | MINOR_* | REQUIRED_VALID_DOCUMENT ...), params (JSON: limit, window kind = rolling|calendar|averaging, window length, averaging period), severity (HARD|SOFT), legalBasis text, appliesTo (contractType, ageGroup, branch, tariffBound flag), validFrom/validTo, version}`. Evaluators are code, one per `type`. Limits, windows and scopes are data. With this design, the 2027 reform (weekly max via agreement, 4-month compensation period, rest −1 h in Gastgewerbe) becomes a new rule-set version with `validFrom = 2027-01-01` and no code change. That is a strong thesis argument.
- Keep **rule-set versions effective-dated** and evaluate a schedule with the rule set valid on each shift's date. Keep **audit logs on rule changes** (Quinyx pattern).
- **Time handling**:
  - Store shift start/end as UTC instants (or `OffsetDateTime`) plus `zone = Europe/Berlin`.
  - Compute durations and rest periods from instants, never from local wall-clock differences.
  - Assign a shift to a "business day" by its local start date. This matters for daily limits, the Sunday count and public holidays: a shift from Sat 18:00 to Sun 02:00 touches a Sunday, so define explicitly whether the Sunday hours count. ArbZG §9 counts Sunday as 00:00–24:00, but this should be confirmed with the legal research notes.
  - On DST nights, a 22:00–06:00 shift lasts 7 h (spring) or 9 h (autumn) of real time. Tests must cover the last Sunday of March and of October. These are the standard EU dates: general knowledge, not fetched here.
  - Reject or explicitly resolve nonexistent local times (spring gap) in the UI, e.g. with `ofStrict`-style validation.
- **Testing**: table-driven unit tests per rule evaluator (boundary cases: exactly 11 h 00 m rest = OK, 10 h 59 m = violation; 6 h 00 m without break vs 6 h 01 m; DST nights; midnight-crossing shifts; leap years for annual Sunday counts; rolling vs calendar window). Add property-based tests if time allows, e.g. "adding a shift never decreases total hours", and solver output re-validated by the validator (generation ⇒ validate(result) has no HARD violations). The community Timefold Python port's `test_constraints.py` shows the pattern of one test per constraint. — [HF Space: test_constraints.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/blackopsrepl/employee-scheduling-python/blob/main/tests/test_constraints.py)

### Gaps
- The final legal text of the ArbZG reform did not exist as of Oct 2026. Whether the 11 h rest period itself changes outside the hospitality reduction, and the exact transition rules, remain open.
- The exact ArbZG/JArbSchG/§43 IfSG rule values (11 h rest, breaks 30/45 min, §11 15 free Sundays, the Belehrung renewal cycle) are not sourced in these notes and should come from the legal-research notes.

## 5. Recommended realistic scope for a 2–5 person student team (one semester) and stretch goal

### Takeaway
Build **validation-first**: employee records with expiring documents/trainings, manual roster planning per branch with demand, a **rule engine that checks ArbZG + JArbSchG + document validity and explains each violation**, an employee mobile-friendly view, open shifts and swap requests that are **blocked** when they would violate a hard rule, and manager overrides with justification and an audit log. Put **auto-generation with CP-SAT or Timefold** in the stretch goal, reusing the same rule definitions. This mirrors where the market is (Planday/Shiftbase/Quinyx validators, Deputy swap blocking) while adding verifiable differentiators (document-expiry eligibility, legal-basis explanations, effective-dated rules for the 2027 reform).

### Cited Findings
- Market baseline features a credible product must have: employee app with real-time shifts, leave requests and open-shift applications ([Papershift](https://www.papershift.com/en/shift-planning)); open shifts restricted to eligible employees, swap/handover/sell with configurable approval cutoffs ([Planday](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/30399-open-shifts-shift-requests-and-shift-swaps)); swap suggestions filtered by availability and qualification, with blocking on compliance issues ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/features/shift-swapping)).
- The validation pattern is proven by commercial tools: immediate re-check on change, a violations counter and per-shift explanation ([Shiftbase](https://help.shiftbase.com/dutch-working-hours-act)); warn with ignore/fix/cancel ([Planday](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/5046320-working-time-rules)).
- The auto-generation building blocks exist with official examples: CP-SAT employee scheduling ([OR-Tools](https://developers.google.com/optimization/scheduling/employee_scheduling)) and soft sequence/sum constraints with penalties ([shift_scheduling_sat.py](https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/stable/examples/python/shift_scheduling_sat.py)); time-limited skills as hard eligibility ([Timefold docs](https://docs.timefold.ai/employee-shift-scheduling/latest/shift-service-constraints/skills-and-risk-factors)).
- Rules are likely to change from 2027 (weekly max via collective agreement, shorter compensation period, rest −1 h in hospitality, electronic recording). — [Handwerksblatt](https://www.handwerksblatt.de/themen-specials/arbeitszeit-erfassen-aber-wie/neues-arbeitszeitgesetz-das-steht-drin); [CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/publication/update-arbeitsrecht-oktober-2026/arbeitsrechtliche-reformen-im-fokus-was-arbeitgeber-jetzt-wissen-muessen)

### Inferences
**MVP (one semester, 2–5 people)**, in priority order:
1. **Master data**: branches, roles/stations (kitchen, service, bar), employees (contract type: Vollzeit/Teilzeit/Minijob/Azubi, date of birth for minors, weekly contract hours, home branch), and documents/trainings with `issuedAt`/`validUntil`. Examples: §43 IfSG Belehrung, Hygieneschulung, Jugendarbeitsschutz-Untersuchung for minors, work permit. Include a dashboard of items expiring in 30/60/90 days.
2. **Demand and shifts**: per branch and day, required headcount per role and time slot; shifts with start/end as instants (Europe/Berlin), role and branch; week view per branch.
3. **Rule engine (validation)**: about 8–12 rule types as data. Examples: min rest between shifts, max daily hours, average hours over a window, break by duration, max consecutive days, min free Sundays per year, minor-specific rules (JArbSchG), required valid document on the shift date, availability/absence conflict, overlap/double booking, demand coverage (under/over).
   - Output: structured violations with rule name, legal basis, measured value vs limit, and affected shifts.
   - UI: per-shift badge with tooltip, violations panel, counter.
   - Publishing with HARD violations requires a manager reason, stored in the audit log.
4. **Employee view**: responsive web or PWA showing own shifts, availability entry, absence requests.
5. **Open shifts and swaps**: employee claims an open shift or requests a swap. The system **pre-validates** and **blocks** the request if any HARD rule fails for the new assignee. Otherwise a manager approves, with an optional auto-approve cutoff (Planday pattern). Notifications can be in-app only.
6. **Tests**: table-driven unit tests per rule including midnight-crossing and DST cases; a seed dataset for one restaurant with about 20 staff.

**Stretch goals** (pick one or two):
- **Auto-generate / auto-fill** using OR-Tools CP-SAT (Python or Java) or Timefold. Hard: all HARD rules plus eligibility; soft: preferences and fairness (Sundays, weekends, late shifts, contract-hours deviation); coverage shortfall heavily penalised. Always re-validate the solver output with the same validator. Start with "fill open shifts in a mostly manual plan" before full generation.
- **"Suggest eligible replacements"** for sick calls or swaps: rank eligible employees by fairness and cost (Deputy pattern). This is cheaper than full optimisation.
- **Fairness report**: per employee per quarter, counts of Sundays, weekends and late shifts, and planned vs contract hours.
- **Rule-set versioning UI**: switch to a hypothetical "ArbZG 2027" rule set and re-validate future schedules.
- **Time-tracking import/export** (planned vs actual) and payroll export (DATEV CSV). These are commonly offered by German gastro tools ([gastromatic pricing](https://www.gastromatic.com/de/preise/); [Softguide: gastromatic](https://www.softguide.de/programm/gastromatic)), but out of MVP scope.

**Explicitly out of scope for the MVP**: payroll, POS integration, native mobile apps (use a PWA), multi-country law, demand forecasting from sales data, ML.

### Gaps
- No source estimating development effort for these features was consulted. The scope above is a judgment based on the market feature set and the algorithm sources, not on measured effort data.
- Whether the restaurant chain is tariff-bound (DEHOGA collective agreement) is unknown. This affects which 2027 rules (weekly maximum, rest reduction) would apply and should be clarified with the client.
